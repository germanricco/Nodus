# What's new in Nodus

What each version brings, newest first. To update, click “Update” when Nodus tells you there's a new version: your data is kept. The versions before 1.10, when Nodus spoke only Spanish, are described in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## 1.12.0

- Screens that answer a question show the answer at the top, large and on its own, like the net worth on Home: an account's balance, what's saved toward a goal, a payee's or tag's total, and what recurring payments cost per month. The other figures go below, in boxes that all look alike, so your eye lands on what matters first.
- A click always leads to the same place: a category, payee or tag opens its page from Home, Analysis or Budgets (it used to open Transactions), and a month's bar opens its transactions. Every row that leads to another page lights up under the mouse, shows the hand and ends in “›”, as accounts do. In Categories, Payees and Tags, where a click chooses, a double click opens.
- The page of a category, payee or tag says when its total is from: what it added up to over the last 12 months, and how many transactions it had in them. All its transactions are still below.
- Deleting an account, a payee or a tag is in the “⋯” menu at the top of its page, as with goals, instead of the red card that sat at the foot. Nodus still asks first and says what goes with it.
- Every list has the same air: a one-line row is as tall in Tags, Categories or Home, and a two-line row (with an initial, a detail under it or a bar) as tall in Transactions, Payees, Accounts, Recurring or Budgets. Payees no longer looks cramped, and the sidebar's accounts sit a little further apart.
- Home's month-by-month cash flow shows a year, as Analysis and a category's page do, instead of six months. Bar charts (Home, Analysis and a category's page) have a scale of round amounts on the left with their guide lines, so values read at a glance.
- Month charts start in the first month with transactions, instead of showing empty months that looked like zero, and the Cash flow chart, on Home and in Analysis, says which color is income and which expenses.
- Month bars show the hand when a click opens that month's transactions, in Analysis and on a category's page; a month with no transactions doesn't. On Home, a month of “Cash flow” now opens its transactions too.
- In a short window, the page of a category, a payee, a tag or a goal scrolls like an account's, instead of squashing the chart and the transactions to fit.
- In a narrow window, or with Help open, the figure boxes move onto a second row instead of squeezing all three together: in Analysis, Budgets, Recurring and Home's “Cash flow”, as an account's page already did. In Analysis, “By category” and “By payee” stack when they don't fit side by side.
- Action buttons, such as “Add transaction” or those at the top of each page, rise a little under the mouse and sink when pressed, as on a web page. With “Reduce animations”, they stay still.
- With the sidebar collapsed, hovering over “Accounts” shows each account's balance and the total, which are otherwise hidden with the list (and it respects the eye that hides amounts).
- On Home, “Spending by category” and “Against the previous period” are one card: the total spent and how much it changed on top, then the six categories that took the most, each with a bar and the percentage of its share, its amount and its change (the rest add up in one line). A click on a category opens its page. The card goes last: cash flow and budgets move up, so you see how much is left without scrolling.
- Transactions reads the way you think of a transaction: what it was, of what kind and how much. Each row has two lines: on top the payee, with the Payees icon so it isn't mistaken for a note, or the note when there is no payee, and below, in gray, the note and the account; with neither, it says “No description” instead of repeating the category; the category with its tags below; and the amount with its sign, in red or green. So nothing gets cut, and dates, categories and amounts always fall in the same column. The payee's initial, in its category's color, helps you find it; transfers and refunds carry their own icon. The same shows in Home's “Latest transactions” (with the date under the amount), on the page of an account, a category, a payee or a tag, which leave out of each row what the page already says, and when choosing a refund's expense.
- A click on the Date, Transaction, Category or Amount title sorts Transactions by that column, and another click turns the order around.
- Above the list, a line says what you are looking at and what it adds up to: “Fun · November 2026 · 2 transactions · -$70,500.00”. With both income and spending, it says how much came in, how much went out and the net.
- The “⋯” menu of a transaction with a payee offers “View payee”, which opens its page.
- What repeats without being a recurring payment is quicker to record: “Duplicate”, in each transaction’s “⋯” menu, opens the form with a copy dated today, and Ctrl+K has “Repeat the last expense”.
- Categories shows how much each one added up to in the period you choose, from the largest down, with a bar of how much it weighs in total expenses or income. Subcategories go under their category, which adds them all up. A search at the top finds a category by its name.
- In Categories, each category forms a group with its subcategories, set apart from the next by a thin line. The arrow on its left hides the subcategories and shows them again; folded, the category says how many it has and its amount still adds them up. Nodus remembers what you folded, and a search shows the subcategory it finds even if its group is folded.
- In Categories, the total of “Expenses” and “Income” sits under its card's title, like a figure in a box, instead of standing alone on the right.
- In Categories, each one has its check box, as in Payees: choose several and merge or delete them together, with a single confirmation.
- Each category has its own page, like accounts and payees: a double click in Categories opens it, as in Payees. It shows how much it added up to over the last 12 months, this month (with the change from the previous month, as on Home) and on average per month, a bar for each month of the last year (a click on a month opens its transactions), its subcategories or the category it sits in, and all its transactions. “Add transaction” from there starts with the category already chosen.
- The page of a category with subcategories goes from the whole to each part: every bar of “Month by month” is split by subcategory, as in Analysis, and the “By subcategory” card shows how much each one added up to over the last 12 months and what share of the total it is; a click opens its page. What sits in the category with no subcategory has a part of its own. When a subcategory has its category's color, the chart uses a lighter or darker shade to tell them apart.
- “Merge with…” joins two categories that are the same: the transactions, recurring payments and payees that had it by default move to the one you choose, and the other is deleted. If the one that stays had no budget, it takes the other's. As when merging payees, the notice offers “Undo” (or Ctrl+Z), and Nodus also saves a copy of your data first.
- Budgets answers first how much you can still spend: at the top, in large type, what's left across all your budgets (or how far over you are), how much that is per day until the end of the month and a bar for the month with today's mark. Below it, only when it happens, which budget went over and how much you spent in categories without a limit, which doesn't come out of what's left; a click brings the “No budget” card up and highlights it for a moment. Rows are cleaner: the name, what's left and the bar; the “% used” shows on hover. A click anywhere on the row, the name or the bar, opens the category, in Budgets and on Home (the bar used to need a double click). Categories without a budget are rows just like them: a click opens the category, its “⋯” menu leads to its transactions and “Set limit” lines up with the amounts above.
- Goals shows each goal as a card: its name with its state, its accounts and date, the amount saved in large type against the target, the bar with its percentage and a sentence about your pace. The whole card lights up and opens the goal with one click. The goals you follow fill the window's width, from what needs attention (overdue, behind) to what you already reached; archived ones go apart, under a title that folds them and remembers your choice.
- Archived accounts fold away just like archived goals: a click on “Archived” hides them to keep Accounts clean, and each page remembers your choice.
- Payees keeps those you pay (“Expenses”) apart from those who pay you (“Income”) with a selector, by their usual category.
- On Payees, each one carries its initial in a circle of its usual category's color (gray without one), so you spot it at a glance. The letter reads well in both themes with any color.
- On Payees and Tags, a click on a column's title sorts by it, and another click turns the order around. The sort arrow used to show, but a click did nothing.
- Payees and Tags have the same period picker as Categories: each list shows how much each one added up to in the period you choose, starting from the current month.
- In Tags and Payees, a row with no transactions in the chosen period but some in others says so in gray (“0 · 3 in total”), so a tag just put on an expense from another month doesn't look unused.
- In Analysis, “By category” says in how many transactions each one was spent, as “By payee” and “By tag” do. The rows of the two cards side by side line up, neither is left with empty space, and on a wide screen both take the whole width instead of leaving a gap on the right.
- In Settings, the rates history shows the last three; “See all” unfolds the rest. And the page of an account, a category, a payee or a tag with no transactions shows its message in a card of its own size, without an empty box down to the bottom.
- Each Help topic has an icon of its own, so they tell apart at a glance.
- More keyboard shortcuts: Ctrl+N records a transaction, Ctrl+F takes you to the page's search (or to Transactions'), Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 open the pages of the sidebar, Alt+← and Alt+→ go back and forward through the pages you visited, and Ctrl+H hides or shows the amounts. Every button with a shortcut tells it when you hover over it, and Help lists them all.
- Nodus can be used entirely from the keyboard. Tab reaches every row of a list and marks it with the same ring as buttons; ↑ and ↓ move from row to row, Enter opens or edits it like a double click, and the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens its “⋯” menu. In Categories, ← folds a group and → unfolds it; in Accounts, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ change an account's order in its group. After each change, the keyboard stays on the same row.
- Ctrl+K opens a search to go anywhere by typing a few letters: a page, an account, a category, a payee, a tag or a goal, or something to do, such as recording a transaction or hiding amounts. With the field empty, what you chose last comes first, and each action shows its shortcut for next time.
- Easier for a screen reader and for the hand: every button that shows only an icon says what it does, every row reads out with its texts, and links, check boxes, switches and the arrow that folds a group measure at least 24 pixels, as the WCAG accessibility standard asks. In forms, Tab goes through the fields top to bottom, colors included.
- Fix: hiding the amounts no longer makes the net worth chart grow. A hidden amount takes the same room as the amount, so the legend below does not rearrange or move anything around it.
- Fix: a button or a row was sometimes highlighted without touching the keyboard: on opening Nodus, closing a dialog, coming back to the window or moving from one page to another, such as the previous month's arrow or “Trends” in Analysis. Now the highlight only shows if you got there with Tab or a shortcut.
- Fix: in a narrow window, the welcome's “Bring my data from another computer” button was cut, and in Settings the row of the rates' source made the page scroll sideways. Buttons that do not fit now go to the line below.
- Fix: in a narrow window, above all with Help open, the header of Transactions, Budgets, Categories, Payees and Tags got cut off. Now the period and the search go under the title when they don't fit beside it, and a long title, such as a category's, ends in “…”.
- Fix: in that same narrow window, each page's content no longer runs out of its place or hides behind a horizontal scroll bar. The Transactions filters move to another line instead of shrinking to “Ac” or “Ca”, tables set aside what matters least (a payee's usual category, a transaction's category), long account, recurring and category names end in “…”, and Settings lays its fields out in two lines.
- Fix: “Last transaction”, on a payee's or a tag's page, showed the date of an installment still to come. Now it shows the latest one that already happened.

## 1.11.0 — 2026-11-21

- Nodus comes to Linux as an AppImage: a single file that opens with a double click on almost any distribution, with nothing to install. It shows up in the applications menu, can open when you sign in and updates itself, as on Windows.
- Nodus has a typeface of its own, Source Sans 3, and looks the same on any computer. It's narrower than before, so lists show more text before cutting it with “…”, like each transaction's description. The hints under each option and the titles of the cards grew a little and are easier to read.
- On Home, Help opens on the list of topics, so you see at once everything it explains. On the other screens it still opens on the topic of that screen.

## 1.10.0 — 2026-11-18

- Nodus speaks English. Choose the language in Settings, under “Language”; it applies when Nodus restarts. Amounts and dates change along with the text: in English they're written “$1,234.56” and “11/18/2026”. The first time, Nodus opens in your Windows language; if you were already using it, it stays in Spanish.
- When you export to CSV, the column names and the separator follow the language, so the file opens correctly in your Excel.
- Fix: the Categories and Tags icons were swapped. Tags now shows a tag, and Categories the “#”.
